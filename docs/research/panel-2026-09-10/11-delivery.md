# Delivering the rules to Iris

## 1. The three rules most likely to cause conflict

- **Rule 3, the 12-SKU cap.** Ten scents are her pride and her creative outlet. A cap reads as "half your work is not good enough," and the retire-one-to-add-one clause turns every new idea into a negotiation. Highest risk.
- **Rule 4, the price floor.** Paw-Fection at a loss is a gift to dogs and her vet. "The catalog rejects it" sounds like "you may not be generous."
- **Rule 5, curated bears.** He is choosing which of her sewing is good enough to show. That is the most personal judgment on the list, aimed at her best and most emotional work.

Rule 8 is a sleeper: painless at launch, explosive at the first quarterly review.

## 2. One reframed sentence per rule

1. "Anything on the site needs the watercolor label and the ingredient panel the law expects, so the site only lists what already has both."
2. "Online shoppers can't pick it up, so every listing gets the same window-light photo setup; I shoot, you make."
3. "Twelve is a shelf size, not a verdict. The site shows twelve; the booth shows everything."
4. "The site has a price floor built in, four times materials, so the beeswax pays for itself. Paw-Fection can be a gift; the site just can't be where you give it."
5. "The bears page shows the four that photograph best and takes a deposit through the form, because custom work gets paid before you cut fabric."
6. "The form closes at one bear a month so no family waits and you never sew grief on a deadline."
7. "Supplies come off the business card, and the card keeps $600 back. Every shop has a rainy-day rule."
8. "Each quarter I pull what sold. Under ten in ninety days rotates off to make room. It can always come back."

## 3. The market-only lane

Give every enthusiasm a named home so "not on the site" never means "no."

- **Booth table.** All ten scents, the postal bear, the Reds bears, samplers, muscle balm. The fair is her stage; the site is the shop window. Say it in those words.
- **Facebook.** Her voice, her puns, her long process posts, untouched. Anything she makes can be posted the day she makes it. Site copy is his; Facebook is hers.
- **"From the Workbench" page.** One page, phone photos allowed, no cart, no prices, "ask at the booth or message Iris." New ideas debut here. It doubles as the audition: ten inquiries earns a catalog slot, which makes rule 3 a promotion path instead of a wall.
- **Vet lane.** Paw-Fection wholesale ($6, 12-pack, prepaid) is her deal with the vet, not a site product. Samples she hands out are hers to give.

## 4. The pre-launch conversation

1. "Mom, the site is your shop window, not your whole workshop. Everything you make still gets made."
2. "The window holds twelve things. The booth and Facebook hold all of it."
3. "I'll run the boring parts: labels, photos, prices, the quarterly numbers. That's my job so it isn't yours."
4. "New ideas go on the Workbench page first. If people ask, it earns a slot."
5. "The bears are the business. One a month, so you never rush a family."
6. "Deal: I decide what's on the site, you decide what gets made?"

## 5. Never say

- "That doesn't sell." Say "that hasn't sold online yet."
- "MLM," "homemade-looking," "cheap," "clutter."
- "The panel said" or "the numbers say" as a shield. He owns the rules.
- "You can't." The site can't; she can.
- "Ten is too many." Say "twelve slots."
- "Without appeal." That phrase stays in his notes and never reaches her ear.
