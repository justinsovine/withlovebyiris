# Memo 02: Markets, booths, and turning table traffic into repeat buyers

Panelist: 15-year booth vendor, Clermont County / east side / NKY circuit.

## 1. Where to set up, and what it costs

Stay within 40 minutes of Bethel. Gas and a 6 a.m. load-in eat margin fast.

- **Bulldog Arts and Crafts Festival, Batavia (Oct 24, 2026).** Clermont home turf, school-run, cheap. Estimate $40 to 75 per 10x10. Apply now.
- **Milford Pottery Holiday Show (Nov 28 to 29).** Two days, right demographic for candles and gift sets. Estimate $75 to 150.
- **Church bazaars, Bethel / Amelia / Williamsburg / Mt. Orab (Oct through early Dec).** $20 to 50 tables. Best fit for scripture candles and memory bears; nobody else there is doing faith-forward bath goods. Ask at her own church first.
- **Crafty Supermarket (Cincinnati, juried, applications closed Sept 1, 2026).** Skip this year, apply July 2027. Fees run roughly $150 to 250 and the crowd is younger and skews away from her brand. Worth one try for reach, not for regulars.
- **Findlay Market outdoor market.** Artisans rank last in priority (agriculture, prepared food, cottage food, then artisan) and they want documentation that materials are sourced locally. Bath base and argan oil will not pass easily. Low odds; do not spend energy here in year one.
- **Hamilton County Fair (June 25 to 28).** $300 for 4 days, no electric. Fair crowds buy $10 impulse items, not $80 bears. Pass.
- **Seasonal farmers markets (Milford, Anderson, Batavia).** Weekly $15 to 35. Good for shampoo bar and paw balm repeat customers who see her every Saturday. Check each market's rule on non-food artisan slots; many cap them.

Budget: 10 to 12 events a year, roughly $800 to 1,200 in fees. If an event does not clear 5x its fee in sales plus signups, drop it.

## 2. Booth-to-site conversion mechanics

The sale at the table is the cheap part. The email is the asset.

- **One QR code, one landing page** (withlovebyiris.com/fair). Not the homepage. It says: "Thanks for stopping by. 10% off your first online order, code BOOTH10, free Bethel pickup." That code is also her attribution tag; every BOOTH10 order came from a fair.
- **"Reorder online" card in every bag.** Business-card size, kraft stock, matching label art, QR on the back. Shampoo bars run out in 6 to 8 weeks. That card is the reorder.
- **Signup at the table: name and phone or email only.** One field too many halves the list. Use a paper clipboard plus a QR to a form. Offer: entry to win a candle drawn at close of show, winner announced by email the next morning. That email is the first touch; it goes to the whole list, not just the winner.
- **Text over email for locals.** A short SMS ("New Key Lime Pie bars, pickup Saturday in Bethel") converts better with this crowd than a newsletter. Keep both.
- **Send the follow-up within 24 hours.** Photo of the booth, link, code. After 48 hours she is forgotten.
- **Local pickup is the hook.** No shipping cost, no rural USPS trip. Offer pickup at a fixed spot and window (her porch, or a church parking lot Saturday morning). Locals will order online for pickup when it costs nothing extra.

## 3. How the site supports the booth

- **One inventory.** Square reader at the booth, site on Medusa. She cannot maintain two counts. Either sync Square to Medusa stock or, simpler, have her mark bars sold out on the phone at end of day. Son should build a one-screen "adjust stock" admin view she can use from the truck.
- **Pre-orders for the next fair.** "Order now, pick up at Bulldog Fest." Zero shipping, guaranteed sale, she packs it the night before. This is the single highest-value custom flow.
- **"Find me at" page.** Upcoming events, dates, booth number. Link it in every bag card and every Facebook post. It also tells the vet's customers where to meet her.
- **Custom order inquiry form** for memory bears and quilts, not a cart. Fields: what garment, photo, deadline, budget. Those are $150 to 400 conversations, not buttons.

## 4. Local B2B placements

- **Vet (Paw-Fection).** Ask for consignment first: they pay nothing up front, she gets 60 to 70% of the $10, restocks monthly. At $10 with 7 real ingredients she is near break-even; raise to $12 and give the vet $4. Do not take a loss to be on a shelf. Also target groomers and the Bethel feed store.
- **Boutiques (Milford Main St, Loveland, Anderson).** Wholesale at 50% or consignment at 60/40 in her favor. Ask for 90 days, monthly settlement, unsold returns. Shampoo bars, balms, candles. Not bears.
- **Funeral homes and hospices (memory bears).** This is the best channel she has. Offer a printed card the funeral director hands to families: "A bear made from their clothes, by a Bethel seamstress." Pay the home nothing; they will refer for goodwill. Hospice of Southwest Ohio, E.C. Nurre, Tufts Schildmeyer are the first calls.
- **Christian bookstores and church gift shops** for scripture candles and men's balms.

## 5. What sells where

At the table: shampoo bars (smell test), $10 paw balm, lip gloss, candles, small gift bags. Under $25, sniffable, giftable.

Online only: memory bears, baby quilts, custom bears, bundles and gift sets, reorders of the same scent. Anything over $50 or that needs a conversation.

Neither: clay masks and scrubs. Nobody buys skincare from a strange tent. Keep them online with samples in the fair bag.

## 6. On Etsy vs local plus direct

The son is mostly right, with one correction. Etsy takes 6.5% transaction plus 3% and $0.25 payment plus listing fees plus 15% offsite ads if it triggers. On $10 paw balm that is brutal, and shampoo bars face thousands of competitors. Local plus direct wins for consumables and for anything with a Bethel pickup story.

The correction: memory bears. Grief buyers search Google and Etsy, not craft fairs. One Etsy listing for memory bears, priced high, with the bulk of fulfillment on her site, brings in customers she will never meet at a bazaar. Etsy as a billboard, not a store. Everything else stays home.
