# Finance and operations memo: With Love by Iris

Panelist: small-business finance and ops coach for solo makers. Numbers marked (est.) are my estimates; the rest are 2026 published figures.

## 1. Legal and ops checklist, in priority order

| # | Item | Cost | Notes |
|---|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Ohio vendor's license (county, fixed location) | $50 one-time (fee rose from $25 in April 2025) | Apply through Clermont County Auditor or Ohio Business Gateway. Required to sell taxable goods from home, including website orders. |
| 2 | Ohio transient vendor's license | $50 one-time | State-issued, valid in all 88 counties. Needed for craft fairs outside Clermont County. Get both. |
| 3 | Product liability insurance | $400 to $515/yr | HSCG member policy: $375 + $25 admin, $1M coverage. ACT Pro for handmade beauty: $515/yr. Do this before the site goes live. Leave-on skin products (balms, lip gloss) and anything sold for pets are the exposure. Many fairs also require a certificate. |
| 4 | EIN | Free (irs.gov, 10 minutes) | Lets her open a business bank account and Stripe without her SSN everywhere. |
| 5 | Separate business checking | $0 (most credit unions) | Non-negotiable. Every Stripe payout, fair cash deposit, and supply purchase goes through it. This alone makes tax time a two-hour job. |
| 6 | LLC | $99 one-time, no Ohio annual report | Yes, but second. Form it once insurance is in place and revenue is real (about $500/mo). At sole-prop scale the insurance is what protects her; the LLC protects the house. File Form 610 online, 3 to 7 business days. |
| 7 | Sales tax collection and filing | 0 cost, ~1 hr per filing | Ohio is origin-based for in-state sales: charge the Bethel/Clermont rate (roughly 6.75% (est.), verify on The Finder) on every Ohio order, whether shipped to Cleveland or picked up. No tax on out-of-state orders until she crosses the $100k economic nexus in another state (she will not). Ohio assigns filing frequency; ask for semiannual. Timely filers keep a 0.75% discount. Medusa: one Ohio tax region, one rate, done. |
| 8 | Stripe | 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction | Set up under the EIN and business account. Turn on Link and Apple Pay. Reader for fairs: Stripe Reader S700 or just Square at the booth (2.6% + $0.15). Two processors is fine; two bank accounts is not. |

Total year-one cost of being legitimate: about $650 to $750. That is one good fair.

## 2. Unit economics

Labor at $20/hr, which is what a skilled maker in Clermont County should pay herself. Card fee 2.9% + $0.30. Shipping subsidy = what she eats when shipping is flat-rate $6 and USPS actually costs more. All COGS are (est.) from her posted ingredients.

| Product | Price | COGS | Labor | Packaging | Card fee | Ship subsidy | Net | Margin |
|---------|-------|------|-------|-----------|----------|--------------|-----|--------|
| Shampoo bar | $12 | $2.25 | $1.50 (4.5 min in batch of 20) | $0.60 kraft + label | $0.65 | $1.50 | $5.50 | 46% |
| Beeswax candle, black tin | $22 | $7.50 (beeswax is $9 to 12/lb) | $2.00 | $1.20 tin + lid label | $0.94 | $2.50 | $7.86 | 36% |
| Paw-Fection balm | $10 | $2.10 | $1.35 | $0.85 tube + label | $0.59 | $2.00 | $3.11 | 31% |
| Memory bear | $150 | $12 (stuffing, thread, photo patch print) | $80 (4 hrs) | $4 gift bag + card | $4.65 | $0 (pickup or customer pays) | $49.35 | 33% |

The paw balm is not actually a loss at $10 if it sells in person. Online it is, once she subsidizes a $6 flat-rate label that costs $5.50 to $9 for a padded mailer. What to do: keep the $10 booth price as a lead-in for the dog crowd, list it online only as a two-pack for $18 or add-on to a cart already above the free-shipping line, and price the vet at wholesale $6 with a 6-unit minimum (she still nets about $1.60 on each with no card fee or shipping). If the vet stocks it, that is her first wholesale account and the template for the next three.

Memory bears are the real business. Four hours at $20 is generous; at $150 she is fine, at $195 with a two-week lead time she is excellent. Never discount these.

## 3. Weekly time budget (25 hrs, solo)

- Production: 10 hrs (batch days, one per product family, Tuesday and Wednesday)
- Fairs: 8 hrs on a fair week (setup, selling, teardown), 0 otherwise; average 4
- Fulfillment: 3 hrs (pack Monday and Thursday only; never daily)
- Content: 3 hrs (one workbench photo session, one long Facebook post repurposed to the site and email)
- Admin: 1 hr (Friday: reconcile bank, log fair cash, check stock)

Automate: order confirmation and shipping emails (Medusa does this), pickup-ready email, sales tax math, a monthly "new batch" email. Drop: custom scent requests under $50, Etsy, replying to every comment individually, hand-writing every shipping label (use Pirate Ship, free, commercial USPS rates).

## 4. 12-month P&L, conservative

Assumptions: 2 fairs/month Apr to Dec, 1 in Jan to Mar (21 fairs), $350 average booth revenue, $45 average booth fee. Online: 6 orders/month in months 1 to 4, 12 in months 5 to 8, 20 in months 9 to 12 at $38 average order. 6 memory bears in the year at $150.

Read that honestly: year one is a paid hobby that covers its costs and pays her about $14.70/hr. Year two, with online at 30 orders/month and 12 bears, clears $20/hr plus a small profit. That is a realistic solo-maker trajectory and beats Etsy, where the 6.5% transaction fee plus $0.20 listings plus 12 to 15% offsite ads would take another $1,500 off this table.

## 5. Inventory and cash

- Hold 4 weeks of stock in bath and body: about 60 shampoo bars (6 per scent, drop the three slowest scents to 3), 24 candles, 20 paw balms, 10 each of the balms and masks. Anything more is cash sitting on a shelf.
- Made-to-order for everything sewn. Memory bears and quilts are inquiry-first: 50% deposit at order, balance at pickup, in Medusa as a custom product with a deposit price.
- Pre-order for candles ahead of the Christmas fairs: open in October, close mid-November.
- Minimum online order: none, but the cart should nudge. Free shipping at $45; that pushes the typical two-bar order to three bars plus a lip gloss and covers the label.
- Cash rule: keep one month of costs (about $600) in the business account and never spend fair cash before it is deposited and logged.

## 6. Three sticky-note metrics

1. **Revenue per fair hour** (booth sales divided by hours including drive and setup). Under $30/hr, drop that fair.
2. **Online orders this month** vs last month. This is the whole point of the site; if it is not rising, the content plan is failing, not the site.
3. **Cash in the business account** at Friday reconcile. If it drops two Fridays in a row, stop buying supplies until it recovers.
