# Panelist memo: the maker's view

From a bath-and-body maker who went craft fair to DTC site. Numbers are 2026 benchmarks where sourced, estimates where marked.

## 1. Product line: cut it in half

Ten shampoo scents is ten photos, ten labels, ten inventory rows, and ten ways to run out of the one people want. Launch the site with **five**: Lavender Fields, Orange Cream, Vanilla Bean, Honeysuckle Dew, and one woodsy bar (merge Sandalwood and Cedarwood into "Cedar & Sandalwood"). Park Tutti Frutti, Key Lime Pie, Sunshine Bliss, Rose of Sharon as a rotating "this season" sixth slot. If her fair sales say a different five, use hers, but the count stays at five.

Lead SKUs: shampoo bars, the two men's balms, beeswax candles. These are the photogenic, giftable, repeatable core.

Keep as cart add-ons: Peppermint Smack lip gloss, Paw-Fection.

Park for launch: clay mask and facial scrub (fold into one "Botanical Face Set" later if demand shows), muscle balm (claims risk, see below), postal bear.

Memory bears and birth quilts are not catalog products. They are inquiry-only custom work at $175 to $300+ (bears) and $250+ (quilts), with a deposit. Give them a page and a form, not a Buy button.

## 2. Pricing

Handmade bars retail $10 to $16 in 2026, with true cost per bar (materials, labor, packaging) of $2.50 to $5. Beeswax runs roughly $10 to $14/lb (estimate). Rule: retail at 4x materials, wholesale at 2x. If 4x feels high, the materials cost is wrong or the product is.

| SKU | Materials (est.) | Retail |
|---|---|---|
| Shampoo bar, ~3 oz | $2.50 to $3.50 (argan and essential oils drive this) | $12, 3 for $32 |
| Beard balm, 2 oz jar | $3 to $4 | $18 |
| Beeswax candle, 8 oz tin | $7 to $8 (7 oz wax, tin, wick) | $24 |
| Lip gloss roll-on | $1 to $1.50 | $6, 3 for $15 |
| Paw-Fection, 2 oz tube | $2.50 to $3.50 | $10 |
| Clay mask, 4 oz (dry) | $2.50 | $14 |

Paw-Fection at $10 is not a loss; it is a 3x product. She does not need to eat margin to keep it "cheap." Price it and let the vet buy at $6 wholesale, minimum 12.

## 3. Compliance: fix before the first online sale

**FDA cosmetic labels (21 CFR 701)** on every bar, balm, mask, scrub, gloss:
- Identity statement ("Shampoo Bar"), not just the scent name.
- Net weight in oz and grams, on the front panel.
- Full ingredient list, descending order, INCI names ("Butyrospermum Parkii (Shea) Butter"), not "shampoo base." She must get the base supplier's INCI list and merge it in.
- Name and address of the business, plus a phone or email for adverse event reports (MoCRA requirement since Dec 2024).
- Round kraft wraps with a watercolor label rarely fit all this. Solve with a second small back label or a hang tag.

**MoCRA**: facilities with under $1M average annual cosmetic sales over three years are exempt from FDA facility registration, product listing, and the GMP rule. She still must keep records of any adverse event, report serious ones to FDA within 15 business days, and be able to show safety substantiation (supplier safety data plus usage-rate documentation is enough at her scale). Fragrance allergen labeling is not final as of this memo; verify before printing large label runs.

**Claims**: "cooling mint muscle balm" is fine; "relieves sore muscles" is a drug claim. Clay mask cannot "detox" or "treat acne." Lip gloss cannot "heal chapped lips." Paw balm is not an FDA cosmetic (animal), but "heals cracked pads" is a veterinary drug claim; "moisturizes, lick-safe ingredients" is the ceiling. Beef tallow in a pet product goes rancid; date it.

**Candles** are CPSC territory, not FDA. Put ASTM F2058 cautionary text on the tin bottom: burn within sight, keep away from things that catch fire, keep away from children and pets, trim wick to 1/4 inch, max burn 3 to 4 hours.

**Business**: get an LLC ($99 in Ohio) and product liability insurance ($300 to $500/yr for makers) before the first shipped order. The vet will ask for the insurance certificate anyway.

## 4. Shelf life, batching, inventory

Anhydrous products (bars, balms, gloss, paw balm): 12 months, limited by the shortest-dated oil. Argan and tallow oxidize; buy small, rotate. Clay mask: ship dry and unpreserved, never pre-wet. Candles: indefinite. Stamp every batch with a lot code (date plus product letter) and log it in a notebook; MoCRA record-keeping and any recall depend on it.

Rhythm for one person: one product family per making day, one making day per week. Batch 24 to 48 bars, 12 to 24 balms, 12 candles. Hold 4 to 6 weeks of stock per SKU, remake at 25% remaining. Set the site's low-stock threshold to match. Photograph the whole batch once, on the same board, same light, same afternoon.

## 5. Repeat consumables

A shampoo bar lasts 4 to 6 weeks. Beard balm 6 to 8 weeks. Lip gloss and paw balm run out in winter. This is the business. Build: a 3-bar bundle at a discount, a "refill" email at day 35 after purchase with a one-click reorder, and a 6-week subscription at 10% off. Candles and bears bring new customers; bars keep them.

## 6. The year-one mistake

Making everything anyone asked for, priced to please the fair table. Every new SKU costs a label, a photo, a supply order, and shelf space in her head. Ten scents, three face products, and a stuffed postal bear is a hobby catalog. Five bars, two balms, one candle, priced at 4x, is a business.
